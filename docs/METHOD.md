# The method: Link, Flow, Grow, and the laws every screen is built to

Kris, 26 September 2026. This is how SPEC, and both products it powers (SiteVIP and Angus Shield), bring a
business from cold to committed, and the test every screen, flow and line of copy is held to. It sits beside the mantra (OUTSIMPLE THEM)
in `CLAUDE.md`, and it is a rule, not background reading. An identical method governs Angus
Shield (its own `docs/METHOD.md`).

## Where it comes from

This was not invented at a desk. It is the method from Kris's 2018 book, *Mindfed*: work that
started in 2004, took about fourteen years to write, and has been worked through in practice across
thirty-plus businesses in the ten years since. SPEC itself (Safety, People, Earnings, Compliance)
came out of that field work; SiteVIP and Angus Shield are the same field-tested method, wearing a
software coat.

## 1. The give-up-point law

**The real enemy is not "they'll do it later". It is that people do nothing.** An empty system goes
nowhere; a system nobody is in does nothing. Every point of friction is a place where someone
quietly gives up, and the whole thing dies there.

So every screen gets one test before it ships, and again after every change:

> **"Where could someone give up here?"** Find it. Remove it.

Hunt them down one by one: a required field they can't fill right now, a step they don't
understand, a screen that asks too much, a word they don't use, a wait with nothing to do.
This is rule 11 ("If it's not simple, it's wrong"), sharpened: not
"could it be simpler?" but "where would a busy owner or a staff member stop and not come back?"

## 2. The bar is love, not tolerance

Each blockage stands between a person and the moment the system was built to give them: feeling
how much their manager cares about them, their KPIs and the rest of it. Clearing blockages is clearing the path to that. The bar is not "they'll put up with it". It is
that an SME owner, and everyone they bring in, **genuinely loves using it**, and we get there by
removing the blockages one at a time.

## 3. Partial data is fine

**Whatever you've got is enough to start.** Getting someone or something into the system matters
more than complete details, because the alternative is that the owner parks it, means to come
back, and never does.

- Incomplete is normal, not an error to block on. Momentum over completeness.
- Ask only for what this step truly needs. Everything else can be filled in later, and the system
  says what is still missing at the moment it matters, with the one step that fixes it.
- Where something is truly needed later, accept the record now and gate the later step that
  needs it, not the first one.
- One of several ways is enough: an email **or** a phone number, never both required.

Blocking someone because you don't have their phone number is not simple.

## 4. Link, Flow, Grow

The whole method, from cold to committed. It maps straight onto where the build's risk is, and the
90-day rollout's phases are named Link and Flow for that reason.

| Phase | What happens | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Link** | Connecting things up and getting people in: the org chart and its people, customers from wherever they live, the financial system. | **Fragile.** High chance of failure; this is where the give-up points bite. |
| **Flow** | The business runs through it every day: jobs, hours, safety, the monthly marks, the money. | The payoff. |
| **Grow** | It becomes how the business decides and grows: the board pack, the 90% rule, people who love their job. | Once here, they never stop. |

**Nearly all of the effort belongs in surviving Link.** Carry a business across into Flow and the
job is essentially done. For SiteVIP, Link is: sign-up, the org chart, inviting people, bringing
in customers and connecting the financial system. Those screens get the hardest give-up-point
hunt of all.
