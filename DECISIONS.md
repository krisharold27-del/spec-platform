# Decisions log

Record every correction or choice made while building. Newest at the bottom.

- 2026-09-06 — Company documents are about SPEC Business Solutions, not the founder personally. Personal goals stay out of this repo.
- 2026-09-06 — Role first, person second: criteria hang off roles; `role_assignments` keeps placement history separately.
- 2026-09-06 — Claude is the interface (guided onboarding, explanations, board output), not a bolt-on feature.
- 2026-09-06 — Scores are computed, never stored. Periods are append-only and lockable.
- 2026-09-06 — Source workbook pillar weights do not always sum to 100% (e.g. 85%, 91%). Engine normalises by applicable weight so it reproduces the workbook; the UI must enforce 100% on save.
- 2026-09-06 — Integrations (job system, accounting, CRM, safety) come after manual/CSV entry works. Phase 4.
- 2026-09-06 — Drizzle ORM instead of Prisma: Prisma needs downloaded query engines, which the build environment could not fetch. Drizzle is pure TypeScript, supports SQLite (dev) and Postgres (prod), and is easier for Claude Code to reason about.
- 2026-09-06 — Team averages are computed over scored roles only. An unscored role is missing data, not a zero; the executive summary says how many roles are scored.
- 2026-09-06 — Local dev needs no accounts: `npm run db:push && npm run db:seed && npm run dev` gives a working demo tenant ("Acme Electrical") on SQLite.
- 2026-09-06 — The four questions are the front door (`/start`); the answers are the tenant's first diagnostic and steer which pillars' KPIs are emphasised.
- 2026-09-06 — Claude registration is a hard gate: nothing in the journey opens until the business confirms a Claude workspace with seats for supervisor level and above.
- 2026-09-06 — Journey step status is computed from data, never ticked by hand.
- 2026-09-06 — Two tiers: Basic (self-serve) and Program (consulting arm on site). Sign-up lands on Basic; Program is requested from the journey page.
- 2026-09-06 — Board output is generated on period lock from the data; Claude rewrites it in plain terms when an API key is present, otherwise the deterministic version stands. Never presents an empty template as a result.
- 2026-09-10 — Scoring engine brought to BUILD_SPEC §3. A blank is Pending, so NA; NA is excluded from both sides of the pillar fraction. A pillar with nothing scorable has no score (null), not 0, and is left out of the role mean. Team % is the mean of role % across scored people. No rounding mid-calculation. Supersedes the 2026-09-06 workbook rule that kept blanks in the denominator; the workbook cases still reproduce because every KPI in them is Y or N.
- 2026-09-10 — Watch scores NA (was N): started, not finished. The guard is `resolveWatchAtLock` — Watch two closed months running becomes Not met on the second lock. Not yet wired into `lockPeriod`; that belongs with the period_scores work (§1.2, §5).
- 2026-09-10 — Locked months keep the answers they were locked with (Watch stored as N) — nothing recalculates history. Open months score from the status. Blanks in an already-locked month now read as NA rather than 0; until period_scores freezes scores at lock, that is the one way the engine change reaches a closed month.
- 2026-09-10 — Access levels: `administrator` · `full` · `readonly`, stored as `readonly` (BUILD_SPEC says `read_only` — the spec should catch up). An administrator manages within scope exactly as `full` does: every management check is `canManage`, never `=== 'full'`, which would lock the GM (the first administrator) out of their own business. Seats and invites still accept `full` too — tightening them to administrator-only is the seats rework (§8.3).
- 2026-09-10 — Sign-in links failed with `otp_expired` ("Email link is invalid or has expired") even when clicked straight away: email security scanners open links to check them, spending a one-time link before the person does. The sign-in email now lands on `/auth/confirm`, a page with one button; the token is spent only when it is pressed. Works on any device, because the token is in the link rather than in the requesting browser's cookie. `next` after sign-in is only ever a path inside SPEC.
- 2026-09-10 — SPEC now sends the sign-in email itself, so no Supabase template change is needed: the service-role client asks Supabase for a token only (`generateLink`, which sends nothing — `invite` when the person has no sign-in identity yet), and Resend sends one email linking to `/auth/confirm`. Without the service-role key or Resend it falls back to Supabase's own email. The sign-in send throws on failure rather than showing "check your email" for an email that never went. Not yet rate-limited by SPEC — Supabase's send limit no longer applies on this path.
- 2026-09-10 — Removed `/admin` "sign in as", which let anyone on ADMIN_EMAILS sign in to any customer's business. BUILD_SPEC §9.1: SPEC as a company has no access and there is no break-glass. The action file is deleted, not just unlinked — a server action is a public endpoint whether or not a button points at it. `/admin` still lists each business's name, plan and GM contact (account data, needed for billing and the Program); its journey-progress column is derived from customer content and is for the founder to decide on.
- 2026-09-10 — Bot protection at sign-up, none of which asks a person to do anything: a trap field people never see; a signed timestamp (a form posted in under 3 seconds, older than 2 hours, or forged is refused); at most 3 new businesses per network address per hour; and Cloudflare Turnstile, which switches on when `NEXT_PUBLIC_TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` and `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` are set. If Cloudflare is unreachable the person is let through and it is logged — a real business is never locked out because a third party is down. Sign-up errors are now messages on the form, keeping the four answers.
- 2026-09-10 — The one-per-minute sign-in email limit is also kept on the person's Supabase sign-in identity (`app_metadata.spec_link_sent_at`), so it holds across servers. Checked before a new token is made, because making one cancels the link already in their inbox.
- 2026-09-10 — "Your board pack is ready" carries no link and no content, not even the business name or the month (§10). `/admin` now shows accounts only — name, plan, start date, administrator, Program requests — and nothing derived from what a customer put into SPEC; the contact comes from the administrator's seat, not the chart. Vendor names removed from the scorecard hint and the schema.
- 2026-09-10 — Incentive rebuilt to §6: seven levels ($4,000 halving to $250; the 6 September `staff` level paid as technician until re-levelled), director outside the scheme; a failure is a pillar under 50% anywhere beneath them (was: a majority of KPIs missed, direct reports only); Sales Ace doubles the ceiling, earned by three consecutive months of both halves, lost by two consecutive failures, never backdated; first period without incentives; a merge pays the higher ceiling once on the mean of the two roles. Pure logic — not yet shown on any screen.
- 2026-09-10 — Regional prices (§8.1) live in `src/lib/pricing.ts`, tested to reduce to 8. Checkout charges the business in its own currency, chosen from the country the hosting platform detects, and picks the Stripe price on the seat product that matches the published amount exactly — a price that has drifted in Stripe is never charged; if none matches it falls back to the configured AUD price and logs it. The price a leader reads in the app comes from the same signal, so it matches what they are charged. Until `tenants.region` exists (§1.1) the detected country is the stand-in for "where the business is". The six regional prices already exist in Stripe test mode; live mode has none yet.
- 2026-09-10 — One email can hold more than one business (a group owner, a consultant, a founder with a sandbox) — the schema always allowed it; sign-in now honours it. Signing in links every seat held under the verified address; the business shown is the one last chosen on that browser (`spec_business` cookie, only ever a business the person has a seat in), else the first joined. "Switch business" appears only to someone with more than one. Signing up with an address already on SPEC now sends the sign-in link straight away instead of a second form asking for the same address.
- 2026-09-10 — The front door is delightfully easy, and the questions come later. Welcome is one line and one button; sign-up is name, business and email; a brand-new business goes straight in with no email step, landing on its own chart. The four questions and the drill-down are asked inside, once, not before entry. Supersedes the entry sequence on screen 1 (The Welcome) — the founder's instruction, 10 September.
- 2026-09-10 — Entering without an email proves nothing about the address, so an address is PROVEN only by signing in through a link sent to it (`app_metadata.spec_email_proven`, set by /auth/confirm and /auth/callback). Seats are linked by email only for a proven address; a sign-up links only the seat it just created. Without this, someone who signed up using another person's address would inherit every seat later given to it. An address already on SPEC always gets a link, never entry.
- 2026-09-10 — Email and password, not sign-in links: "stop sending the link — I will lose everyone like this." Sign-up is name, business, email and password, straight in, no email; sign-in is email and password. Email is sent for exactly two things: "Forgot password?" (a set-password link) and confirming the address the first time someone adds a seat. Security arrives when it matters — adding seats and connecting systems — never at the door. Sign-in attempts are limited to ten per address per network per fifteen minutes. Supersedes the magic-link sign-in of 6–10 September.
- 2026-09-10 — Production functions moved from iad1 (US East) to syd1 (Sydney), beside the database: every query had been crossing the Pacific, and sign-up took 30 seconds. Setting up a business now writes each role's KPIs in one batch instead of one row at a time. Buttons grey out while working, so nothing is submitted twice.
- 2026-09-10 — Security pass before opening sign-ups: every server action that takes an id from a form now checks it belongs to the caller's business (gates, board approval, role change, scorecard KPIs); KPI setup obeys scope; billing (checkout, portal) and the board pack need an administrator or the top of the chart. The app connects with a database role that bypasses RLS, so these application checks are the isolation — RLS is defence in depth only.
- 2026-09-11 — SPEC Today (`/today`) is the page everybody starts the work day on, and sign-in lands there; someone holding no role is passed on to the journey. Every block is derived from what the business recorded — pillar lights, what needs you, direct reports, connected feeds, changes, training — with the scope resolved once at the top so nothing on the page can reach a role the viewer is not entitled to see. The Ask panel computes its answers from the person's own card: no model call, no key, no write, per "read to inform, never write to change". Messages links out to the person's own mail and holds no content. The one write is the weekly meeting — a date, never what was said, and logging twice in a week is a no-op.
- 2026-09-11 — The whole app moves onto the Organic design system: tailwind.config.ts is the token sheet (cream ground, sand surfaces, terracotta accent, sage second accent, 100–900 ramps), Caprasimo over Figtree through next/font, containers over-rounded and controls pilled. The four pillar colours were retuned onto the same warm axis — terracotta, sage, ochre, mulberry — because the old blue (#169BD5) and violet (#8064A2) read as borrowed on a cream page. A separate `light` scale carries the traffic lights, and its pending step is a warm neutral: "pending is never red" is now enforced by the palette rather than by remembering.
- 2026-09-11 — Training is modelled properly rather than borrowed from the Compliance pillar, which was showing governance KPIs ("Monthly board output delivered on time") as though they were somebody's training. Three tables: `training_modules` (SPEC's own catalogue, per tenant), `role_curriculum` (which modules a role requires) and `training_records` (one person's progress through one module). The path belongs to the ROLE — role first, person second, so a role may require training with nobody in it and reassigning somebody never edits it. Progress belongs to the PERSON, because a person learns a module once. Sign-off belongs to the PLACEMENT (`role_assignments.trained_at` / `trained_by`), so it does not travel with somebody who moves to a different job. Sign-off stays a manager's decision: the bar reaching the end makes a person *ready* to be signed off, never signed off. Seat of the catalogue is `seed/training_modules.json`, installed at provision with a starting path per role level — SPEC proposes, the leader edits. Overdue training reaches "what needs me today" because it reaches the board pack regardless of scores.
- 2026-09-11 — Ace eligibility is reported as three separate steps — path complete, signed off, three closed months at 90% — rather than one verdict, so a person who has done two of them is told which one is outstanding. `lib/training.aceSteps` says where somebody stands; `lib/incentive` and BUILD_SPEC §6.4 still govern the money, and this changes neither. The later instruction ("trained on the job, minimum 90% on the KPI board three months in a row") is recorded here for the spec to catch up: §6.4's current rule is outcome plus behaviours, which is not the same test.
- 2026-09-11 — Local development runs on a Postgres on the developer's own machine, with `DATABASE_URL` in `.env.local` (git-ignored, and it takes precedence over `.env`) so `db:push` can never be aimed at the hosted database by accident. README's setup was still describing the SQLite `data/dev.db` this repo stopped using; it now describes what actually works. `npm test` needs no database at all — every rule in `src/lib` is pure.
- 2026-09-11 — Basic and Advanced, decided by one question to the leader ("do you want the power of AI?"), stored on `tenants.tier` and defaulting to `basic`. Basic has no connectors and no assistant: every number typed in and confirmed by a name. It is a complete way to run the whole system rather than a crippled one — no feature anywhere is reachable only by connecting something — and it costs the same, because it is the same system. Nothing switches itself on for a business that has not asked for it.
- 2026-09-11 — The weekly meeting generates its own agenda from the month as it stands: a failing hard gate first, then the number that moved the wrong way, then work that is late, then a miss nobody explained, then a vacancy. Three is a ceiling and what did not fit is counted rather than dropped. `meetings` gains attendance and decisions as their own columns — a senior meeting the senior group did not attend is a note, and a decision outlives the week it was made in. The read on the week is deterministic, so the same month always produces the same record.
- 2026-09-11 — `assessment_periods.status` gains `submitted` between open and locked, with submitted_by/at and signed_by/at. Submitting says "finished as far as I am concerned"; signing says "the business accepts it"; locking follows the signature rather than a date. Submitting is not a trap — a submitted month is reopened and corrected, and only a LOCKED month is corrected by dated amendment, because only a locked month is history.
- 2026-09-11 — Monthly scoring reads provenance, not just results: fed by a connected system, confirmed by a person, waiting for a name, or not tracked at all. A fed row is read-only — hand-editing it would break the one thing that makes a fed number worth more than a typed one. A row claiming a source that is NOT connected is deliberately not treated as fed; that is the case where a number would silently stop updating with nobody noticing. Flags block on a miss with no reason and on a manual number nobody confirmed, and deliberately do NOT block on an honestly reported gap: blocking that would push people to invent numbers.
- 2026-09-11 — Approvals is one queue, and most of it is derived rather than filed — a month waiting to be signed is a period with status `submitted`, a path waiting for a manager is a finished path with no signature against it. Derived items cannot drift out of step with the business and nobody has to remember to close them. Only decisions needing a record of their own (a board approving a connector, money, a renegotiated target) are stored in `approvals`. Sorted by how long each has waited, never by importance: SPEC does not rank one person's decision above another's.
- 2026-09-11 — The org chart's layout is computed — one recursive walk assigning a centre x and a depth row, absolutely positioned cards, and three kinds of connector rectangle. Nested flex columns produced unconnected lines and a recursion bug and must not come back. Anything the walk never reaches (a cycle, a branch under a detached role) is placed as a root of its own: a chart must never render blank because one reporting line is wrong. Dragging a card moves the role and everything under it; dragging a name moves the person and swaps if the destination is filled, because silently vacating loses a placement nobody asked to lose.
- 2026-09-11 — Connectors are by category and never by vendor, which is also what makes board approval meaningful: a business cannot route around it by using a product SPEC has never heard of. Financial and people systems raise a board request carrying the exact data scope in plain words, sit disconnected until it is decided, and the server refuses to mark one live without an approval behind it. A decline is a real outcome — the system stays off and its KPIs stay manual.
- 2026-09-11 — The permission matrix in `lib/permissions` is a DESCRIPTION of what the server enforces, laid out so an administrator can read it in one go. It is never consulted before a write; hiding a button was never access control. Two permissions are undelegable at any level, including by an administrator acting for themselves: approving a sensitive connector, and signing off a period. A checklist role always sees its own card in full — being measured by something you are not allowed to read would be indefensible.
- 2026-09-11 — People answers both halves from the org chart rather than a separate HR silo. Clear to Work is pass or fail per person and reads "not established" where nothing has been recorded, because assuming clear when nobody checked is the exact failure the gate exists to prevent. The cost of a vacancy is written in pillars and orphaned roles, never in dollars — SPEC does not know what the work was worth, and a made-up figure would undermine every real number on the page. Candidates are rated against the four pillars, and an unrated pillar is left out rather than counted as zero.
- 2026-09-11 — The hiring checks, the sector measures and the training catalogue are deliberately generic. The handoff designs named one client's licences, pay bands, awards, advertising channels and software vendors; hardcoding any of those would make SPEC fit exactly one customer. What is fixed is the SHAPE — that each check has an answer, that each pillar is measured by something, that each role starts with core modules — and the specifics belong to the business. Tests assert no industry or vendor leaks back in.
- 2026-09-11 — The labour calculator claims 10–30% of the LABOUR component, never of revenue: labour is the part a business controls month to month. It shows both percentages and the revenue one is always smaller. Every line can be switched off and switching one off removes it from the headline and every figure; recovery rates are published per line rather than rolled into one number, so each is arguable. No line names a problem without naming what SPEC does about it.
- 2026-09-11 — The front door stays one line and one button (the founder's instruction of 10 September). The fuller argument lives at `/pricing` and `/sectors`, linked from it rather than folded back into it. The Landing design in the September handoff is therefore implemented as those two pages, not as a rebuilt `/welcome`.
- 2026-09-11 — `/site` is the on-site view: four targets and nothing else, on plain form posts so there is no bundle to download before the first tap works on one bar of signal. Reporting a near miss is open to anybody with a seat including readonly — one only a manager can file is one that does not get filed — and it lands as an action on this week's meeting with a name against it.
