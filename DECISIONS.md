- 2026-09-11 — The shallow J curve is a named design principle and a measured feature, not a claim. Every transformation dips before it climbs, and the dip is deep because discovery is manual; SPEC's mechanism is specific — when a business's own systems feed the KPIs, the picture exists the day they connect, so the engagement starts at execution instead of at mystery-solving. `/curve` measures each business's own phases from its own records and is deliberately able to say no: a business whose discovery ran longer than the benchmark is told so, because a page that can only congratulate the product is not a measurement. - 2026-09-11 — Conversation boards (`/boards`) are a distinct object from both the board of directors and a dashboard: a visual built to provoke one specific realisation from data the business already recorded, so a hard conversation has a neutral third party in it. Three rules, all enforced in code and tested. **The board never labels the person** — the first one worked because a director recognised himself in his own numbers, and a board that says "you are doing this" is the consultant confronting him again wearing a chart; it is also the rule book's instruction never to name somebody's psychology back at them. The word from the original brief is therefore in the source comments as its origin and never on screen. **A board is only ever about the person asking for it** — there is no roleId parameter and no way to pass one, because the moment it can be pointed at somebody it stops being a mirror and becomes surveillance. **Every board carries the one change that would move it**, since a realisation with no next action is just bad news about yourself. Comparison is always against a described way of working, never against another person or business — a conversation board is exactly where a leaderboard would be most tempting and most damaging. The first three: who decides here, who is actually in the room, and where things wait. The sharpest signal is targets agreed exactly as proposed, because SPEC's own claim is that a target is agreed and never imposed; if none ever moved, none was.

- 2026-09-11 — The **Board Charter**: four commitments, one per pillar, that do not change between businesses and are not negotiated. Zero harm physical or mental; never lose great talent; gross profit at the percentage this business needs to be profitable; no breach of the commitments the business has already made. Each is carried by a HARD GATE rather than by a criterion inside a pillar's percentage — the existing Zero Harm mechanism generalised rather than a new one invented, so a commitment is pass or fail beside the score and never averaged into it. A commitment three good measures sitting next to it can dilute is not a commitment, and a business that hurt somebody has not had a 94% month. Safety deliberately reuses the `zero_harm` gate businesses already enter, because SPEC never re-asks for what it has been told. Gate keys are strings, so this needed no schema change at all.
- 2026-09-11 — The charter splits on BASIS, and that split is the whole idea. Three commitments are ABSOLUTE: their figure is nought and is never derived from what the business has been managing, because averaging a year of injuries to set an achievable target is a statement about how many people you expect to hurt. Exactly one is EARNED — gross profit — and it is earned precisely because it is the only one of the four that is a fact about this business rather than a line every business holds. SPEC cannot supply it: a margin taken from anywhere else is either comfortable enough to be useless or high enough to be ignored.
- 2026-09-11 — A target is judged SOUND or not, which is a different question from whether it was agreed. `lib/targets.soundness` reads every target against the median of the business's own last twelve closed months — the median, not the mean, so one shutdown or one enormous job cannot reset what everybody is held to. At or below the actual is TOO EASY: met by carrying on exactly as before, quietly adding a green light to every month while nothing improves. Up to a fifth above is SOUND. Beyond that is OUT OF REACH: missed every month, and a measure that is always red stops being read — after which the rest of the card is read the same way. Under six closed months SPEC declines to judge and says so, because a confident answer from evidence that does not support one is worse than no answer. `MAX_STRETCH` (20%) and `MIN_MONTHS_TO_JUDGE` (6) are published constants, arguable like the J curve's discovery benchmark.
- 2026-09-11 — **A target of nought is a commitment, not a stretch, wherever it appears.** The stretch band is relative and nothing is within a fifth of nought, so a purely relative test declared every zero target out of reach — including "zero incidents" and "zero breaches". Telling a business its zero-harm target is unrealistic would be the worst sentence this product could produce. Zero targets get the charter's exemption at role level too. The exemption is for nought itself and not for every small number: a ceiling the business is nowhere near is a real finding and is still reported as one.

- 2026-09-11 — `/api/health` checks the database's SHAPE, not just that it answers. A deploy can succeed, the app can start, `select 1` can pass, and the product can still be broken because the build shipped with a column the database has not been given — a 500 on some pages and silence on the others, which is the hardest failure to diagnose and the easiest to ship. The expectation is derived FROM the Drizzle definitions rather than written out by hand: a second list of columns drifts the first time somebody adds one, and a drifted check reports healthy, which is worse than no check. The comparison is deliberately one-directional — a column the database has and the code does not is what every rollback and mid-migration state looks like, so flagging it would cry wolf on exactly the deploys somebody is already watching. It reads `information_schema` only, touches no tenant row, and returns no values, so it stays safe to expose to an operator who may not look at a customer's numbers. Proven in both directions against real databases before shipping: 503 naming the missing table and column against a database one release behind, 200 against a correct one.
- 2026-09-11 — The database is brought up to the build AUTOMATICALLY, in the build step (`scripts/deploy-migrate.ts`, wired into `npm run build`). This replaces asking a person to run a database migration from a command line at the moment a release goes out — not a reasonable thing to ask of anybody, the step most likely to be forgotten, and forgetting it looks like the product being broken rather than like a missed step. The migration journal could not be used for this: it had one entry against twelve SQL files, because the files were hand-written and `push` was always the real mechanism. **The script adds and refuses to drop.** A schema push reconciles in both directions, and dropping a column is how an automated deploy turns into data loss that no test would catch — so if the database holds anything this build does not know about, it stops, prints the list, changes nothing and fails the deploy, because that is a question for a person. A missing `DATABASE_URL` is not a failure and lets the build through, since the public pages need no database and taking the marketing site down over a missing setting would be a worse outcome than the gap `/api/health` already reports. An unreachable database fails loudly. Proven against real databases in all six states before shipping: behind (migrates, 288 assessments and 42 criteria intact), already correct (no-op), holding an unknown table and column while behind (refuses, both survive), holding extras but not behind (no push, so no risk), unconfigured (build continues), unreachable (build fails).
- 2026-09-11 — **The deploy-time migration can never fail a build, and the build command survives the migration tool being absent entirely.** The first version of it exited non-zero when it could not reach the database or found something it would not touch — and that took a production deploy down, for a reason invisible from outside the build sandbox. `tsx` and `drizzle-kit` are devDependencies, so a build environment with `NODE_ENV=production` prunes them and the first command dies before anything is even attempted. Two corrections: the script now returns rather than exiting on every unhappy path, and the build script uses `npm run db:migrate || true; next build` so neither a missing tool nor a failed migration can stop a release. Nothing is hidden by this — everything the migration declines to do is reported by `/api/health` as a 503 naming the exact tables and columns. The lesson generalises: a helper that brings the database forward must never be able to stop the thing it was written to help.
- 2026-09-11 — **The repository had no automated checks at all, and that is the root cause of every surprise this project has had.** Every safeguard was somebody remembering to run it, and "verified locally, with a database, with dev dependencies installed" is not what a deploy environment looks like. `.github/workflows/ci.yml` now runs on every pull request and every push to main, and it deliberately reproduces the conditions that BROKE rather than the ones that work: typecheck, the full test suite, a build with **no** database configured, a build against an **empty** database (proving a new environment can create its schema from nothing), the same build again (proving the migration is idempotent), and then it starts the built app and asks it for pages — because "it compiled" and "it serves" are different claims and only the second one matters.
- 2026-09-11 — **`src/db` connects on first use, not on import.** It used to throw at module load when `DATABASE_URL` was missing, and almost every module imports `db` — so one absent setting stopped the production build while Next.js collected page data for an API route, and at runtime killed every page including the marketing ones that never touch a database. Connecting lazily puts the failure where it belongs: a page that reads nothing serves normally, a route that queries fails on that request, and `/api/health` names the missing setting. A Proxy keeps every call site unchanged, so this is a change of failure behaviour rather than something anybody has to write code around. **The CI above caught this on its first run.**
- 2026-09-11 — **Nothing on the request path may throw because an auth service is missing or slow.** `createServerClient(process.env.X!, ...)` threw "Your project's URL and Key are required" from inside a page render, surfacing as a 500 on every protected page; the middleware's unguarded `getUser()` would do the same during a Supabase outage, taking the marketing site down with it. `createClient()` now returns null when unconfigured — which makes the compiler force every caller to decide what the absence means — and the middleware is wrapped, bounded to three seconds, and returns the response regardless. Reading who is signed in treats an absent service as NOBODY, which is the only honest answer and sends the visitor to `/signin` instead of to an error page. Anything that WRITES — signing in, signing up, sending a link — still fails visibly, because silently doing nothing there would be worse. Verified with Supabase entirely unset: public pages 200, sign-in and sign-up 200, every protected page 307 to sign-in, nothing 500.
- 2026-09-11 — `tests/resilience.test.ts` holds these properties, including by reading the source as text for `process.env.X!`, a `try`/`catch` in the request-path middleware, and `process.exit(1)` in the deploy migration. Crude on purpose: these are one-character regressions that read as tidying up, pass review, and take the site down the next time a setting is rotated.
- 2026-09-11 — The brand arrives: the feathertail glider mascot and the SPEC mark, from the second design export (which also added Mascot, Logo Concepts and Colour System — three designs that did not exist in the first handoff). **The mark is built as a component, not shipped as a picture**, because the logo design is not a static logo: the O is the outcome and the clock, the claw is the hand, and where the hand sits is where the score sits. `SpecMark` takes a score and draws the ring travelling red → amber → green with the claw pivoting to match; at 100% the ring closes, the hand lands on twelve and stays, because a business that has finished does not go back to the beginning. `score={null}` is the brand at rest — the snapped state used everywhere outside the product. One object serves the favicon, both navigation headers, and any place a month is shown, which is the design's own instruction: "one idea rather than a logo and a separate set of charts". The C2PA provenance block was stripped from `logo.svg` (8,675 → 898 bytes) because it is served on every page. The glider sits on the front door beside the supporting line it illustrates, "nimble and powerful".
- 2026-09-11 — The front-door guard now counts the WORDS a visitor reads rather than lines of source. Adding the mascot and a two-column layout tripped a line-count test while the page still said thirty-four words; a guard that fires on layout gets its limit raised until it means nothing. The instruction being protected is "one line and one button", which is about the amount of argument on the page, so that is what is now measured.
- 2026-09-11 — **Option D, decided and applied: the pillar is a letter, and colour is only ever the score.** Inside the product a pillar carries no colour of its own — it is S, P, E or C — and the only thing coloured is how it is going: green, amber, red. The reason is that two colour systems on one card can contradict each other: a People card painted sage as its identity, with a red score inside it, shows two greens and a red meaning three different things, and a reader has to learn which green is which before they can read their own business. With one system that cannot happen, and it is colour-blind safe because the letter carries the identity. This reverses the four-pillar palette recorded on 11 September; the colours are **moved, not deleted** — `BRAND_COLOUR` still holds them for the logo, the public site (`/how`, `/sectors`, `/start`) and the printed board pack, which are the places SPEC presents itself rather than showing a business its own numbers. `PILLAR_META` lost its `colour` key entirely so the wrong thing is hard to reach, and `tests/colour.test.ts` reads the source as text to keep the four hexes out of every other file.
- 2026-09-11 — Colour uses the 90% rule (`scoreColour`), deliberately NOT `band()`. `band()` calls a pillar "on track" only at 100%, because a pillar is a set of measures and every one should be met — right for a LABEL, wrong for a light: a business holding 94% would see amber on every card while being told it is at the standard, which is the exact contradiction Option D exists to remove. The two live side by side on purpose.
- 2026-09-11 — **The login trap is closed.** The sign-in is created before the business, deliberately, so an address already in use is refused before anything is built — which left a gap: if anything after it failed, somebody owned an account attached to nothing. They could authenticate, every page found no seat and sent them to `/signin`, and `/signin` showed the form again. An unbreakable loop, with no message, at the exact moment a new customer decides whether to trust the product. Three changes close it: sign-up RESUMES when the account exists but no seat does (proving the password first, because an address must never be claimable by filling in a form); `/signin` detects the stranded state and sends them somewhere that can finish the job; and an already-signed-in person is sent into the product rather than shown a form that reads as though the last one failed. `tests/signup-resume.test.ts` holds all three.
- 2026-09-11 — **You do not pay for a house before seeing inside it.** `/look` builds a real tenant on the spot — org chart, four questions, a month part-marked with one pillar deliberately behind — hands the browser a token for it, and drops the visitor straight onto the chart. Nobody is asked for an email, a password or a card. Signing up afterwards KEEPS that business rather than starting them over: `claimLook` renames it and clears the token. The safety rests on three things: the token is 256 bits of randomness that exists nowhere but that tenant's own row, so it cannot be pointed at anybody else's business; it is deleted the moment a real person claims the tenant, which is what stops an old browser reaching a business once it belongs to a customer; and a visitor is READ-ONLY, enforced in `assertWritable`, the one chokepoint every write already passes through — which also means a visitor can never send an email, invite anybody or reach anything that bills.
- 2026-09-11 — The visitor sits in the top role's actual seat rather than being a user with no role, because a roleless viewer gets bounced off Today to the journey — the screen for a business still being built, and the opposite of what somebody came to see. A consequence worth writing down: their viewer then looks exactly like a customer's, so nothing about the USER distinguishes a look-around. `Shell` asks `lib/look` directly instead. A visitor is also offered "Finish looking" rather than "Sign out", since they never signed in.
- 2026-09-11 — Saying no is a real button that goes somewhere gracious, with no second ask, no discount and nothing to cancel. A product whose whole argument is that it tells a business the truth cannot start the relationship by being sticky — and an ask with only one comfortable exit is a trap that people can feel.
