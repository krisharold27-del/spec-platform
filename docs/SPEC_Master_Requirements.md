## 1. What SPEC is

SPEC — Safety, People, Earnings, Compliance — is the proprietary GM-to-board operating system owned by **SPEC Business Solutions**. It was developed by the founder from 2012, run manually on Excel for ~15 years across roughly 30 rollouts, and is now being productised for SMEs. The company is positioned as established since 2012 and has traded under this name since at least early 2025.

Guiding principles, in priority order: make money; do it safely; ensure all staff love their job and are amazing at it. Compliance and governance exist to protect those three, not as a competing fourth priority.

The diagnostic is four yes/no questions — are you going well in Safety, People, Earnings, Compliance? — that isolate the single hurting pillar, then drill within it (incidents vs unhappy clients vs something else) to the specific fix. The client self-diagnoses before ever contacting the company.

Origins: Linkpad (2012, interactive org chart + KPIs, built with an IT partner, didn't take off). SPEC is the same idea refined for 15 years. The name change matters because Linkpad had no obvious purpose to a business leader; the four SPEC questions do.

**Target outcome for the client:** after a one-year engagement, a functioning, self-sustaining operation where people know their jobs — so the SPEC spend is a one-off investment to fix the system, not an ongoing cost.

## 2. The product vision (as stated 6 Sept 2026)

A business signs up on its own. Claude guides them — training in using the system, what to enter, what happens next. The leader (GM) enters business expectations and the top of the org chart. Then the cascade: the GM defines the roles reporting to them and assigns people; each manager does the same for their reports; the KPI system builds downward. Everyone lands on a dashboard of the four pillars, each with the KPIs relevant to their role.

**Non-negotiable design rules**

1. Role first, person second. Roles are defined by what the business needs; the person is then assigned to the role. The role is never modified to fit the person.
2. Claude is the core of the product, not a feature. Guidance, training, "what next", "why this KPI" — all Claude, reasoning from SPEC principles so the owner has an independent source of trust in the plan (not just relaying what the consultant said).
3. "If it's not simple, it's wrong."
4. Solutions, not commentary. Every report of a miss comes with a proposed fix.
5. The chassis transfers; only org-specific questions and target numbers get rebuilt per org.

**Target outcome of provisioning:** the same "clear plan within about two days" result the founder's Excel method produces manually, generated automatically from the company name plus the rule book.

## 3. Access model

- Full access: supervisor level and above (GM, managers, supervisors) — define roles, set KPIs, score, write notes into KPIs.
- Read-only: staff below supervisor (estimators, admin, field staff) — see their own role's pillars, KPI status, and outstanding items.
- JBI sizing: ~40 staff, 10 full, 30 read-only. Read-only staff do not need Claude Enterprise seats; a shared dashboard generated by the full-access users covers them.
- Staff-level view is task-level checklist items per pillar (done / not done / outstanding), not abstract scores — e.g. monthly hazard report submitted.

## 4. Scoring engine

Source: SPEC_Workbook_Full "How To Use This System", SPEC_Master_Scorecard_Professional.

**Layer 1 — Team Behavioural Scorecard.** Per role, per pillar: weighted criteria answered Y / N / NA.
`pillar score = Σ(weight where Y) / Σ(weight where answer ≠ NA)`. Person score = mean of four pillars. Team score = mean across people per pillar. Weights per pillar sum to 100%.

**Layer 2 — Executive Summary hard gates** (pass/fail, reported separately):
- Zero Harm: any LTI / MTI / psychosocial incident above 0 is red. No partial credit.
- Clear to Work: training compliance must be 100% for "YES". Not a target — a standard.

**The 90% rule.** A business "is SPEC" only when all four pillars hold at 90%+ for two consecutive months. One good month is noise.

**Snapshots are append-only.** Each month is a new dated assessment; earlier rows are never overwritten (History Log / PBI_Assessments pattern).

**KPI structure per role.** Two KPIs per pillar, eight per role, negotiated with the role holder (e.g. Jordan's GP target proposed at 42%, agreed at 40%).

**Existing relational schema** (from the Master Scorecard's Power BI tables): PBI_People (PersonID, Person, Active), PBI_Categories (CategoryID, Category, SortOrder, Colour — Safety #E67E22, People #5B9E3F, Earnings #169BD5, Compliance #8064A2), PBI_Assessments (AssessmentDate, PersonID, CategoryID, CriterionID, Criterion, Weight, Answer, ApplicableFlag, AchievedFlag, WeightedAchieved).

## 5. The rule book — rollout sequence

This is the sequence the provisioning flow must encode. Source: SPEC_Workbook_Full tabs, Command Center, chat.

**Before day one**
- Question Zero: why did the business bring in a GM / SPEC now — what was the specific moment or number? If leadership can't answer clearly, that is itself information.
- Organisational diagnostic: what does the organisation do well today (with evidence)? What does it want to do well in years 1, 2, 3? Same thing or different thing? Same → reinforce and scale; different → protect the strength while building the new capability.
- Financial Truth Matrix: is the business functioning / are the financials functioning (four quadrants); within financials, are the numbers bad or is the reporting of the numbers bad?

**Week one — First Meeting: Leadership Vision & Alignment**
- What success looks like (how the owner would spend their time; what a week looks like when they've stepped back).
- Commercial reality: how the cost base is calculated; are compliance costs in pricing; is pricing adjusted for running safely; modelled margin impact; biggest gap between perceived and actual profitability.
- Cost & revenue coverage checklist — rate understanding of every element.
- A realistic timeline: results between 3 and 6–12 months; minimum viable term six months; generally sorted by twelve.
- What gives confidence / what builds trust / how we'll communicate.
- Systems in use (feeds the data-sources tab).
- NPS baseline.
- What we're agreeing to; early warning signs named now; ongoing expectations log.
- Success and failure definition; the four failure shapes and countermeasures; the J-curve trough; competitive advantage on four fronts (retain talent, defend against poaching, defend against customer drift, understand growth regions).

**Weeks one–two — structure**
- Org chart built as roles reporting to roles. COGS streams under the GM: Commercial (commercial manager — numbers correct and delivered ASAP: weekly GP, monthly net profit), Operations (manager plus supervisors signed off as capable — billable hours and safety), Growth (retain existing clients, win new ones).
- Three-tier P&L: Operations judged on gross-profit-based operational EBITDA (revenue − direct job costs − controllable ops overhead, excluding management salaries); GM judged on controllable cash net profit (before depreciation, amortisation, interest, corporate allocations, one-offs; systems running cost in, systems training cost out); owner/board on full statutory P&L. Commercial manager produces these as three standing monthly reports, plus a weekly P&L to gross profit.

**Weeks three–four — KPIs**
- Two KPIs per pillar per role, negotiated. Safety KPIs mirror across roles as an organisational standard (zero incidents, zero workers comp claims).
- Commercial manager Earnings KPI includes the STAR rating: Systems, Truth, Accuracy, Results — one to five stars monthly for financial reporting confidence, assigned by the GM via a short checklist (numbers on time, clean reconciliation, matches on-the-ground reality, no unexplained variances) plus a one-line reason. Fix is monthly then weekly financial visibility instead of quarterly.
- Early-warning levers, in order of go-live: (1) billables / gross profit — GP above 42% target, billables presented weekly; if weekly billables ≤85%, commercial manager produces impact report and revised monthly forecast; (2) invoicing accuracy — Simpro vs Xero variance, 1% threshold; (3) pipeline conversion — only once segmented by sector.
- Power meter: prospecting power from CRM/job data — client visits, new work, calls, quotes, activity, leads; weighted score across interactions, leads and lead value; target 90%; shared across all scorecards but weighted 80% to the growth role. Client-base coverage component: every client ever invoiced ÷ addressable market (working denominator 1,000), tiered by annual spend ($1M+, $200K–1M, $50–200K, <$50K).

**Ongoing rhythm**
- Weekly SOG / COGS meeting (Tuesday): solutions-focused; billables presented; when the power meter is low, the meeting finds the fix.
- Daily notes log (small file); monthly consolidation into the master workbook; monthly board output: each pillar vs 90%, gate status, one headline driver per pillar, plain-terms summary (going well / needs attention / what we need from the owner), data-integrity flags, product progress.
- Monthly incentives unlocked by completing the role's SPEC KPIs (not by certification): GM $4,000, Manager $2,000, Supervisor $1,000, Staff $500.

**Onboarding checklist per client:** before day one, day one, month one, month three, month six, month twelve. Repeatable, not bespoke.

## 6. Cross-client learnings loop

After each engagement, capture learnings as explicit rules — pattern identified → prescribed action — with names and financials stripped. The rule set feeds the next provisioning so each rollout is faster than the last. It must cover the core setup itself: how to set up the org chart, build KPIs, set GP targets, structure meetings, and the leadership buy-in diagnostic.

**Leadership buy-in diagnostic.** Across ~30 rollouts outcomes split roughly 50/50 into total success or outright failure. The deciding factor is the owner/most senior leader: success when they're bought in and follow through; failure when they meddle or refuse to fix an identified problem. Curtis Electrical is documented as a readiness signal, not a failure; a Perth plumbing company engagement failed when the owner couldn't cope.

## 7. Systems and integrations (phase 4)

Target stack per client mirrors JBI: job management (Simpro), financials (Xero), safety/compliance (Safety Minder), CRM (HubSpot), productivity (Microsoft 365), with Claude as the connecting layer. Pillar coverage: Safety ← Safety Minder; Earnings ← Xero + CRM; Compliance ← Simpro + Safety Minder; People fundamentals ← Simpro (sentiment/development deferred). Never assume a connection is live; verify. Manual/CSV entry must work before any connector exists.

Related capabilities discussed: AI estimator (align CRM, jobs and financials to capture inputs like an estimator's spreadsheet, then validate estimates against actuals); invoice fraud monitoring (Claude flags anomalies on every invoice — new payee details, round amounts, same person raising and approving — owner reviews the flagged list).

## 8. Certification and training module (phase 5)

Three tracks: SPEC Manager (Basic — setting SPEC KPIs; Intermediate — managing them; Advanced — applying neuroscience/brain-function processes to management), SPEC Supervisor, SPEC Team Leader. SPEC-branded standard, not RTO/Cert IV. Delivered by a company master trainer (the founder initially) on a tiered per-sign-off fee. Known constraint: trainers who understand both SPEC and its neuroscience basis are hard to find, so the module must be built to let the company certify its own trainers over time. Business-leader want served: certainty that all staff are certified.

## 9. Commercial model

- **Front door:** the four questions — do you have safety issues? people issues? earnings issues? compliance issues? A yes to any means the business needs the system; then it learns why, which is what the company helps with.
- **Try first, then Program:** businesses use the Basic self-serve system (target shape ~1,000 businesses at about $100/year), with an option to move to a SPEC Program — rollout and training delivered by the consulting arm, with the principal attending site and running the system (target ~20 active programs).
- Delivery tiers: Self-Serve; Board-Level Control Process; Embedded Senior Manager. Virtual-GM framing: $10K/month base (Claude-led daily GM function + company principal at board level), $20K/month integrated (principal on-site one day a week), stepping down after 6–12 months to a $5K/month board-attendance retainer. Anchored against a top-tier GM salary (~$250K) so the client sees top-tier GM calibre at roughly half the cost of hiring one.
- Cost-neutral pitch: SPEC finds and redirects what the business is already wasting (reference client example: ~$300K/year misallocated marketing and salary spend pays for a full year).
- Hooks: "stop guessing"; "are you distressed, or is your business distressed?"; "don't bother hiring a GM, just use SPEC". Framework: the four questions every struggling owner silently asks → SPEC solves it → start SOG meetings.
- Target market: ~100 named businesses in Victoria / southern NSW, $8M–$30M revenue, 50–250 staff, visible pain signals; 10 conversions. Entry via accountants and accounting groups by region, in logistics, supply chain and electrical. Market sizing confirmed as not a constraint (top ~2% by revenue across electrical, plumbing and logistics is several thousand businesses).
- Capacity model: the company is not targeting volume — roughly 4–5 clients at the integrated tier or ~10 at the base tier. Making ~60% of the GM function virtual lets one principal run 3–4 engagements concurrently; adding principals scales from there.
- Launch budget: website $4–8K (or DIY), $50–250/month hosting, LinkedIn ads $2–5K/month.
- Naming ideas in play: "SPEC Cyclops" (structure that channels raw power); SPEC is the engine, COGS is the gearbox.
- Additional service line: training, assessment and certification of client staff (section 8).

## 10. Constraints

- No former employer or client names on the website or any public material. Clients are 100% confidential; references are private and offered only to serious prospects.
- Product repo carries no client data; each client is its own tenant.
- SPEC principles can be described publicly only in generic terms; nothing proprietary (weighting, sequence, diagnostics) is given away.
- Nothing lives only in a chat transcript — if it isn't in Drive or the project, it isn't done.

## 11. JBI as the reference implementation

JBI Electrical is the company's first client engagement pairing SPEC with Claude, and the live test bed for the platform. The engagement is on a $25K/month budget and its rollout speed has been the proof point for productising.

- Roles: GM (the company's principal, embedded) → Jordan Head of Operations, Anthony Head of Growth/BD, Jason Head of Commercial. Jordan has two supervisors (Wangaratta, Cobram); Anthony two estimators; Jason admin staff.
- Jordan's locked scorecard — Safety: zero incidents, zero workers comp claims. People: zero negative turnover, supervisor development pathway fulfilled. Earnings: billable utilisation >85%, GP 40%. Compliance: zero breaches, NPS 80+.
- Anthony's locked scorecard — Safety: as above. People: client NPS >80, department 100% staffed (hires or AI-covered). Earnings: quote-to-win 25%, monthly revenue >$900K. Compliance: contractual obligations met, 25% of work under contract.
- Jason: KPIs pending his return (third week of September); includes STAR rating and the three-report structure.
- The GM scorecard and the three linked manager scorecards were built in a Claude project using Artifacts as the dashboard layer — this is the working prototype of the product's display layer. Target: all four scorecards complete by end of September 2026.
- Known data issues to fix before September rollover: two files named SPEC_Daily_Notes_Log (fork), 14 days unlogged, workbook Current Month still Aug 2026.

## 12. What the platform is not yet

A multi-org portfolio layer (one row per client, portfolio-level exception flagging, Claude synthesising across each org's own logged entries) does not exist and is a separate build once JBI proves the model.
